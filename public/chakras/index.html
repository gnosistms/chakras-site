Where are the Chakras?

In this essay we ask a simple question: where exactly is each chakra in the human body? When we read the works of Samael Aun Weor with this question in mind, we find a large number of apparent contradictions. Most of them can be resolved by a more careful reading of Samael Aun Weor and of the texts he cites. Click here to see a 3D visualization of our results.

This is not a clairvoyant esoteric investigation. Our goal here is simply to answer the question: “What do the texts say?” In places where there is ambiguity in the texts, we selected the interpretation that is supported by the majority of books and lectures published in the later years of Samael Aun Weor's lifetime. In a minority of cases where even that was not sufficient to settle the question, we selected what seemed the most logical interpretation. We encourage our readers to examine our references and make their own conclusions.

The key is a picture from C. W. Leadbeater's book The Chakras (1927). Leadbeater describes each chakra as a flower that opens at the surface of the body, with a stem that runs inward and joins the spinal column. So when an author gives the location of a chakra, he may mean the opening of the flower on the outside, the point where the stem joins the spine, or an organ the stem passes through on its way between the two. Once we keep in mind that the flower, the stem and the root of each chakra lie in three different places, many of the apparent contradictions resolve without difficulty.

The sources themselves do not all agree, however. Leadbeater says that a chakra's stem does not join the spine directly behind its flower, but at a point several centimetres higher: the stem is “usually curving downwards”. He is the only author who describes the chakras this way, and the others, Samael Aun Weor included, do so only when they quote or follow him. Sivananda, by contrast, places each chakra in the spine at the same height as the region of the body it belongs to.

Samael Aun Weor draws on Leadbeater, Sivananda, M. (the author of The Dayspring of Youth) and a few other sources. So whenever we read one of his descriptions, we have to ask which picture it follows: Leadbeater's, which we call the drooping-stem model, or the traditional one, in which each chakra is simply associated with the organs near its opening and at the same height, which we call the level model. We also have to ask whether a sentence about a chakra, or about one of the seven churches of the Revelation of Saint John, which he places along the spine, refers to the flower, the stem or the root. With those two questions in hand, we are ready to make sense of the mystery.

Our view

Samael Aun Weor places the Muladhara between the anus and the genitals, at the perineum, as Sivananda does. A few late passages put the lotus “in the coccygeal bone”. Read with the flower, stem and root in mind, these describe the root, which is in the coccyx. The flower opens at the perineum, facing down. When these authors say “the genitals” or “the sexual organs” without saying whose, they mean a man's, and “between the anus and the genitals” is measured on the male body. In a woman the same place is the perineum just behind the vaginal opening, not midway between the anus and the vaginal opening.

Root · where the stem joins the spine
S4, the fourth sacral vertebra

Our view

Only Leadbeater describes a stem that joins the spine, so the root is where he puts it, in Table II of The Chakras. Samael Aun Weor repeats his list in The Perfect Matrimony, chapter 19 (1961). Leadbeater gives the fourth sacral vertebra, which fits his description of the root in “the last real cell or hollow of the spine close to the coccyx”, the lower end of the sacral canal. In about 75 other statements, from 1954 to 1978, Samael Aun Weor places the spinal centre, the Church of Ephesus, “in the coccyx”. That tells us where the centre lies (the level model), not where a stem joins. Sivananda's “second vertebra of the coccygeal region” marks where he believed the spinal cord ends; it actually ends near the first lumbar vertebra.

Flower · where the flower opens
Front of the body, a few cm above the pubic bone (at the height of the uterus in women)

Our view

Samael Aun Weor gives the height: “at the height of the prostate in men and of the uterus in women”. The prostate lies behind the pubic bone and the uterus a little above it, so the flower opens on the front of the body a few centimetres above the pubic bone, in both men and women; in women, at the height of the uterus. The other authors put the lotus “at the root” or “at the base” of the generative organs. We read this as where the penis meets the abdomen on its upper side, the same place in a woman's body, not the underside near the perineum, where the Muladhara is. One list that puts it “two fingers below the solar plexus” (Mayan Mysteries, 1977) is taken as a slip.

Our view

Only Leadbeater describes a stem that joins the spine, so the root is where he puts it, in Table II of The Chakras. Samael Aun Weor repeats his list in The Perfect Matrimony, chapter 19 (1961). There the heart chakra is “over the heart, eighth cervical”. There is no eighth cervical vertebra; Leadbeater means the eighth cervical nerve, which leaves the spine between C7 and T1. His later statements place the centre “at the height of the heart”, which is a height, not a root.

How the passages were read

Only Leadbeater has drooping stems

Of the authors Samael Aun Weor draws on, only Leadbeater describes chakras whose stems join the spine at a root and droop down towards the opening: “In each case an etheric stem, usually curving downwards, connects this root in the spine with the external chakra.” Jorge Adoum mentions “the stems of all the vertebral centres”, but in a passage copied almost word for word from the Spanish translation of The Chakras. Whenever Samael Aun Weor speaks of stems, or of fine threads joining the chakras to the spine, he is drawing on Leadbeater. He copies him directly once, in the vertebra list of The Perfect Matrimony, chapter 19, and that book names him: “Mr. Leadbeater describes them in great detail.” He never says the stems droop. Every other author relates a chakra to the spine by position only.

So the roots follow Leadbeater

Where the stem joins the spine is a question only the drooping-stem model asks, so the roots are Leadbeater's, unless Samael Aun Weor explicitly corrects him or names the joining point in words that cannot mean simply “at the same height”. None of his statements does. He corrects Leadbeater once, on the second chakra (“the true second centre is the prostatic and not the splenic”), without naming a vertebra, so the Svadhisthana has no root vertebra.

The flower, the stem and the root

A passage that says what the lotus grows out of (it “emanates from”, “sprouts from” or “has its root in” a gland) is about the stem, not about where the flower opens. A passage that places the lotus in its own gland (“the fiery lotus of your pituitary gland”) names the gland it belongs to; the same book can put that lotus between the eyebrows. A passage about what a chakra controls (“it controls the kidneys”) says what it governs, not where it lies.

The churches

Samael Aun Weor places the seven churches of the Revelation along the spine, and usually a church is the spinal centre of its chakra. He does not use the word the same way everywhere, so each passage about a church was read in its context.

Dates

Where Samael Aun Weor's statements differ, his later works (from 1966 on) were given more weight. The Perfect Matrimony was first published in 1950 and greatly expanded for an edition finished in 1961; each passage from it is dated by whether it already appears in the 1950 text. The chapter 19 vertebra list does not, so it dates from 1961.
